Concept

Order is the proof: every story the app tells (a rule going red then green, an attempt failing then being fixed, a quote leading to a cancel) is a numbered sequence read top to bottom, and nothing can be read out of turn.

The governing idea was written by the creative-director agent, and David adopted it as his concept. It was not written by him; this is recorded for EFF-9.

The proposition

What the territory refuses

  1. A two-axis matrix as the way into a story: the rule page and the cancel screen read down one spine, and the matrix is only an index.
  2. Wall-clock time axes, waterfalls and duration bars: position is ordinal (#1, #2, ...), because the walkthrough and matrix carry commit order, not commit dates, and API responses carry no timestamps.
  3. Side-by-side panes and tabs that let a story's steps be read out of order.
  4. Folding retries into a counter or a passing summary: every attempt keeps its own rung.
  5. Asking the user to re-enter a value a later step depends on: the quoted date is carried down, not typed again.

Layout

Single vertical spine of numbered rungs: an ordinal rail down the page with each step's label to its left and its receipt to its right; retries step off to the side and rejoin the spine; the keyboard moves rung to rung with the keys shown; at 390 wide the rail moves to the left edge and receipts stack under their labels.

Tone

Procedural and stepwise: a numbered protocol with short imperative step labels, one thing at a time.

Type: T5, licence checked

Both faces are OFL-1.1 in their Google Fonts builds, so they can be self-hosted from a public repo. The research's "EULA" was the Braille Institute's own download; the kit uses the Google Fonts OFL builds, and their OFL.txt ships beside the font files.
